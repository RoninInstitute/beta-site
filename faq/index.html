    Frequently Asked Questions


    Your questions about RIIS 2.0 answered.

    © 2026 JLDeLoatch

    This FAQ contains answers to some common questions. It is a simplified guide. In case of any conflict, the RIIS 2.0 Bylaws and adopted policies govern.

    About us

    What is RIIS 2.0?

    The Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0 (RIIS 2.0) is a California nonprofit public benefit corporation recognized tax exempt  under section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue code. Its mission is to support and facilitate the execution and dissemination of scholarly research in the public interest. RIIS 2.0 builds on the values of truth, empathy, belonging, and open scholarship, offering independent scholars a community outside traditional academia. Our Research Organization Registry (ROR) ID is: https://ror.org/000xkb537 

    Why start RIIS 2.0?

    RIIS 2.0 was created in part as a response to a scholarly system that treats tenure-track faculty positions as the only legitimate path, despite the fact that many talented scholars cannot or do not want to follow that route. While this results in a great deal of wasted expertise, the problem runs deeper: a system that values people primarily for their productivity is often at odds with empathy and human flourishing. RIIS 2.0 starts from the premise that scholarship is not one-size-fits-all, and that meaningful contributions can occur at many levels of intensity, including part-time and fractional engagement. By supporting diverse, flexible models of scholarly life, RIIS 2.0 seeks not only to broaden participation in knowledge production, but to support scholars as whole people, with lives, values, and constraints that matter.

    Why are we called RIIS 2.0?

    There was a previous organization legally named Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship, (see the FAQ below) (often referred to informally as the Ronin Institute) that dissolved in September 2024. RIIS 2.0 was subsequently incorporated as a new nonprofit in California in April 2025.

    As this was not a continuation of the former organization, there was no transfer of assets or identity from the prior organization. Simply reverting to the original name and logo was not an option. Our new organization had to establish its own name and visual identity. This also meant that we could not reuse the former organization’s logo, and we needed a name that distinguishes the new entity from the former one, hence Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0, or RIIS 2.0 for short. At the same time, we intentionally kept the name close to the original to reflect the continuity in membership, mission and purpose.

    What fields does RIIS 2.0 represent?

    All of them! Our Research Scholars hail from all across the academic spectrum, from physics to biology to history to philosophy to religion and poetry. Please see our current directory of scholars at: https://ronininstitute.org/scholars to get a sense of the breadth of fields and scholarly contributions from RIIS 2.0 Research Scholars.

    Do you have departments?

    No. Many of our Research Scholars decided to pursue independent scholarship specifically because their research interests did not align well with a traditional academic department. As our numbers grow, so do our “affinity groups,” which are groups of Research Scholars with overlapping and complementary interests. Unlike departments, these labels are descriptive, not prescriptive.

    Does RIIS 2.0 support research grant administration or fiscal sponsorship?

    Not at the moment. RIIS 2.0 is currently focused on establishing an institute based on  volunteer administrative capacity. We are not able to serve as a U.S. applicant organization, fiscal sponsor, or grant administrator for any external funding program. Even “administrative only” arrangements, including fiscal sponsorship require legal, financial, and compliance infrastructure that we do not yet have fully in place. In the future, as the organization matures, we may be able to expand into these areas.

    Can RIIS 2.0 act as a research ethics board?

    No. RIIS 2.0 is a scholarly affiliation organization and does not provide research ethics review such as an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or IACUC review. Affiliates are independently responsible for all required research compliance and regulatory approvals.

    Does RIIS 2.0 offer access to digital library resources?

    Unfortunately, no. Access to library resources remains one of the most significant challenges to the independent scholar. Access to most paid library resources, such as databases and paywalled journals, far exceeds the resources of many smaller institutions, including RIIS 2.0.

    Affiliation

    What benefits will I gain as a member?

    Most importantly, you will have voting rights as a Fellow and a say in the type of institute that RIIS 2.0 becomes, having an impact on the future of independent scholarship and its role in greater society. You will be able to use “Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0 (RIIS 2.0)” as an affiliation on any of your academic work, including papers, proposals, presentations, signatures, etc., but RIIS 2.0 does not endorse individual work; responsibility for content rests with the author. You will also be connected with other RIIS 2.0 research scholars to be part of a community where we can exchange and share knowledge, through our new RIIS 2.0 Discord workspace and community events (mostly online).

    We also currently offer all Fellows RIIS 2.0 email addresses with full inboxes within the @ronininstitute.org domain allowing them to send and receive email, as well as an entry in our scholars directory:     https://ronininstitute.org/scholars/. We hope to soon offer individual profile pages as well. As we are still a very new institute, we will be relying on our inaugural members to create the other benefits we hope to offer. Please join us as an Affiliate to help us build RIIS 2.0 and figure out what these will be!

    How can I join RIIS 2.0?

    We currently have one Affiliate status at RIIS 2.0: Fellow (Fellows also have the title Research Scholar). To become a Fellow:

    1. Review our eligibility requirements below.
    2. Join our update mailing list, where we will announce applications and provide links to the join form.
    3. Attend an event! We require all applicants to attend at least one RIIS 2.0 event and meet other Fellows before applying to get a sense of what RIIS 2.0 is all about. Some examples of events include attending office hours, or another meeting with other RIIS 2.0 Fellows. If you require accommodation in lieu of attending an event, please contact us at .
    4. Submit an application, using the RIIS 2.0 Fellow Application Form.  Please ensure you have attended an event, or have made other accommodations before you submit your application. We will not review applications unless the applicant has done so.
    5. Once your application is approved (see the FAQ “What happens after I submit my application”, below for the process), there are annual membership dues of $100 USD (at the time of writing). Note that the membership period is 12 months from the date you join. You will receive an renewal invoice 30 days before the membership dues are due for the following year.  For example if you join April 21, your membership year will run from April 21 to April 20 of the following year. In that following year, you will receive an invoice on March 21 that will be due April 20.

    If you have other questions about the process, contact us directly at .

    What are the eligibility requirements for joining RIIS 2.0?

    Here are the eligibility requirements for joining RIIS 2.0 as a Fellow:

    • Minimum academic standard: Must demonstrate academic achievement and scholarly commitment equivalent to at least one year of study at an accredited university
    • Scholarly intent: Must be actively pursuing scholarship. This means having a clear, good-faith intention to pursue scholarly work (broadly defined) at any intensity. We are not focused on traditional academic measures of productivity and scholarship can take many forms.
    • Age requirement: Must be 18 years or older
    • Good standing: Must not have been denied or removed from RIIS 2.0 or a similar research institute previously due to proven issues related to behavior, ethics, or misconduct.
    • Submit an application, which is reviewed by the Affiliation Working Group (see below), which then makes a recommendation to the Council. Each application is reviewed by at least two members of the Working Group.
    • Agree to and sign the Affiliation Agreement, which includes:
    • Belonging Policy
    • Code of Conduct
    • Conflict Resolution Policy
    • Terms of Service
    • Privacy Policy
    What happens after I submit my application?
    • You will first receive an automated reply with a copy of your electronically signed agreement, typically within minutes (please also check your spam folder, just in case it was misidentified).
    • Applications are reviewed periodically. You should receive a notice of the status of your application within approximately 60 days of your submission. Once your application is approved, we will send an invoice, which includes information about how to pay membership dues.
    • Once payment has been received into our bank account, you will receive your official welcome email, which will include details on completing the onboarding process. Please note that if you pay electronically it may take an additional four or five business days for us to verify the transaction and transfer it into our bank account.
    Why are you charging membership dues?

    RIIS 2.0 charges membership dues for Fellows to ensure organizational sustainability and member accountability, while preserving the nonprofit’s mission of supporting independent scholarship. This rationale is grounded in both practical needs and governance principles, as reflected in RIIS 2.0’s bylaws and policies. Maintaining RIIS 2.0 costs money, including:

    • Website hosting and digital platforms (e.g., Zoom, domain name, email, file storage)
    • State and federal incorporation and annual compliance fees
    • Insurance
    • Legal services
    • Accounting services

    In the previous NJ-based Ronin Institute, some of these costs (such as digital platforms) were paid directly by members of that Board, and thus, affiliates were heavily subsidised. In alignment with our Belonging Policy and our commitment to equitable access to scholarship, upon request, affiliates from World Bank-defined Low & Middle Income countries may be able to join at a discounted rate. If this applies to you, please state this in your membership application. 

    The membership period runs for 12 months from the date you join.

    I was a member of the New Jersey-based Ronin Institute, am I still an Affiliate?

    No. RIIS 2.0 is a new California nonprofit public benefit corporation, with a separate legal identity, governance structure, and membership process (ROR ID: https://ror.org/000xkb537). Affiliation with RIIS 2.0 requires submitting an application (see above), being formally accepted, and paying dues.  

    All currently accepted Fellows are listed on our Scholars directory: https://ronininstitute.org/scholars/ If you are not on this directory, then you are very likely not yet part of RIIS 2.0.

    The New Jersey-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit “Ronin Institute” was incorporated in 2012, and ceased operations in 2024 (see the questions below under “Background and history” for more details). Membership in this former, now defunct, NJ-based organization does not automatically confer affiliation with RIIS 2.0.  However, the process of joining RIIS 2.0 for previous Research Scholars should be straightforward and similar to the old process that existed at the time of dissolution, so we encourage you to apply!

    Can I continue to use the old “Ronin Institute” as an affiliation on papers or profiles like LinkedIn?

    No. As the NJ-based organization no longer exists, it is incorrect to continue to list it as an active affiliation. Accurate institutional affiliation is important not only for RIIS 2.0, but also for individual scholars. Listing an incorrect or defunct affiliation can raise red flags with journals, funders, collaborators, and indexing services, potentially undermining credibility or creating confusion. We want to help everyone avoid these unintended consequences.

    If you already have a LinkedIn profile that lists your affiliation with the old NJ-based organization, please also read the question below about how to update your profile so it does not list a defunct organization as an active affiliation. You can continue to list it as a former affiliation with an end date of 2024.

    How do I update my LinkedIn profile?

    If you are a current Fellow who has formally joined RIIS 2.0 (application approved and dues paid) you should add a position under “Experience” using the new RIIS 2.0 LinkedIn page: https://www.linkedin.com/company/riis2, with the start date of when you were accepted as a Fellow (it must be no earlier than April 2025, when the new organization was launched).

    If you have a position under “Experience” with the old New Jersey LinkedIn profile, you should update that position with an end date in 2024, as the organization dissolved itself in that year, and currently has no affiliates. Conversely, until you have formally joined, you should not add or update to include RIIS 2.0 as your current affiliation.

    Here are some concrete examples of correct and incorrect usage of listing your position as an affiliation under “Experience”:

    Former member NJ-based org.

    Then later joined RIIS 2.0.

    ✅

    Fellow joined RIIS 2.0.

    Not a previous member of NJ-based org

    Start date later than April 2025

    ✅

    Former member of NJ-based org.

    Has not yet joined RIIS 2.0.

    End date is in 2024

    ❌
    Not correct: 

    RIIS 2.0 did not exist until April 2025.

    Needs to be split into two positions under Experiences

    ❌
    Not correct:

    No end date for NJ organization.

    Needs to add an end date.

    Whether or not you are a current Fellow, we encourage everyone to follow https://www.linkedin.com/company/riis2 to help build visibility and awareness of RIIS 2.0.

    Do Affiliates have different ranks?

    No. When you join RIIS 2.0 as a dues-paying member, you are designated a Fellow with full membership voting and participation rights. Non-dues-paying Associates may attend community events and working groups, but are non-voting and do not vote in Council or Fellowship decisions. All Affiliates also have the title “Research Scholar”.

    Do Affiliates hold other affiliations?

    Yes, in some cases they do. Research Scholars can hold dual (or multiple) affiliations with traditional academic/research institutions, or with other companies or organizations (like IGDORE). Holding multiple affiliations is consistent with our mission: facilitating scholarship in whatever mode works best for each individual Research Scholar. In many cases, holding a traditional academic or other appointment alongside their Research Scholar position may be the best way for a scholar to support their research.

    How do I use this affiliation, for example, in a publication?

    We recommend using the full legal name as your affiliation, e.g.

                Your Name, Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0

    If  this is too long, or if an abbreviation is permitted, the following are acceptable alternatives:

                Ronin Institute (RIIS 2.0)
            RIIS 2.0

    We strongly advise against using “Ronin Institute" without qualification, to avoid confusion with the former NJ-based organization. If an affiliation requires a physical address, please use the city, state, and zip code form (do not use the street, most journals no longer require street information):

            Sacramento, CA 95816 USA

    If the journal or organization insists on including the street, you can use the full legal address, but we advise against routinely including the street information, unless absolutely necessary:

            2108 N St STE N, Sacramento, CA 95816 USA

    What if my work requires large equipment or lab space?

    RIIS 2.0 does not possess physical lab space. Some Research Scholars whose work involves laboratory-based experiments also have an affiliation with a university or research institution with those facilities. Others work with collaborators who have access to lab space. In certain metropolitan areas, there are now also facilities where you can rent access to lab equipment on a short-term basis (e.g., bio-hacker spaces).

    Governance

    How is RIIS 2.0 governed?

    RIIS 2.0 is governed by a Council that serves as the corporation’s board of directors under California nonprofit law. Under the current Bylaws, the Council may consist of up to fifteen Fellows and no fewer than three, with the authorized number established by Council resolution In addition, Working Groups advise the Council and help run key aspects of RIIS 2.0, but ultimate responsibility resides with the Council as required by California law. Since the organization was only just created, it does not yet have an elected Council, but an Interim Council (see question below) is serving until the first Council election.

    What is a Working Group?

    A Working Group (WG) consists of a group of Fellows (often including at least one Council member) in good standing. Working Groups advise the Council and help run key aspects of RIIS 2.0, but ultimate responsibility resides with the Council. We are in the process of creating several standing Working Groups. At the moment, we have an Affiliation Working Group (which handles applications for affiliation and overall membership policy), a Research Working Group (which handles issues related to research), an Infrastructure Working Group (for our digital technology), a Financial Working Group, and a Volunteer Coordination Working Group (which oversees elections and tries to match Fellows stated interests with volunteer opportunities with RIIS 2.0). All WGs are volunteer and run by Fellows; there is no separate paid administrative staff. Working Groups are open to any Fellow willing to commit to helping run that key aspect of the organization.

    Who is part of the Affiliation Working Group?

    Current members of the Affiliation Working Group are Mohammad Alanzi, Jovita De Loatch, Nick Jackson, John LaRocco, Alex Lancaster, Rami Saydjari, Carolyn Sealfon, Anne Thessen, and Arika Virapongse.

    Who makes administrative decisions and who makes scholarly/academic decisions at RIIS 2.0?

    Fellows do both! RIIS 2.0 consists only of Fellows, i.e., Research Scholars. Fellows thus comprise both the administration (via the Council and Working Groups) and are all scholars themselves. RIIS 2.0 is self-governed by its Fellows and currently relies substantially on membership dues and volunteer effort to support its operations. RIIS 2.0 currently operates primarily through volunteer Fellows and does not presently maintain a paid administrative staff.

    Decision-making

    How are decisions made?

    Decision-making is documented in Council minutes and working group notes. The Council generally seeks consensus. When consensus cannot be achieved, or where law or the Bylaws require a formal vote, the Council proceeds by vote under the applicable voting rules.

    How are RIIS 2.0 Council elections conducted?

    Elections are governed by the RIIS 2.0 bylaws. The Volunteer Coordination Working Group (VCWG) administers the process. Candidates may be nominated by Fellows in good standing and have at least one year of Fellowship or comparable leadership experience. A slate is developed for representation, but additional nominations are allowed. Voting is electronic, using a secure, anonymous platform.

    How are votes made electronically?

    Votes may be taken live during meetings, asynchronously over a fourteen-day window, or by other secure methods. All votes must meet the quorum and are recorded for transparency.

    Background and history

    What is the relationship between the former New Jersey-based Ronin Institute and the new RIIS 2.0?

    The Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0 (RIIS 2.0) is an entirely new, legally distinct entity with completely new leadership and not a continuation of the former New Jersey-based Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship, which existed from 2012-2024. While we were able to purchase and reuse the old domain name of ronininstitute.org, RIIS 2.0 is a new, independent nonprofit that carries forward the spirit of independent scholarship fostered by the former Ronin Institute, but with a distinct governance structure, legal status, and institutional direction. It is not a legal successor, but rather a community-led reinvention of the original mission — with an effort to be more inclusive, transparent, and sustainable.

    • Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship 2.0 (RIIS 2.0) is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, recognized as tax-exempt under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. It was incorporated on 2025/04/23 under EIN 33-4776448.
    • The original Ronin Institute for Independent Scholarship was a New Jersey-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit incorporated in 2012 that is now defunct, having ceased operations and legally dissolved itself in September 2024 and relinquished or shut down key infrastructure (e.g., Slack, email, and website hosting) and finances and filed a final tax return in February 2025.
    • There is no legal continuity between the two entities. RIIS 2.0 has its own Articles of Incorporation, bylaws, EIN, and a governing Council. For this reason, if you have issues you need addressed with the now dissolved New Jersey-based Ronin Institute, we are unable to address them and encourage you to seek qualified legal advice.
    Do Research Scholars of the former NJ-based Institute automatically become Affiliates of RIIS 2.0?

    No. See the answer to the question “I was a member of the New Jersey-based Ronin Institute, am I still an Affiliate?”, above.  

    How did RIIS 2.0 form?
    • Bylaws group: A group of Research Scholars, who were not connected to the Board of Directors of the now-dissolved NJ-based Ronin Institute, formed a bylaws group to explore creating a new set of bylaws that could be the basis of either a continuation of the old Ronin Institute or a new organization for Research Scholars. The group was open to any former member. Meetings were open to all former members of the now-dissolved NJ-based Ronin Institute and colleagues, and all discussions were reported in the bylaws group meeting notes. 
    • Survey conducted: The by-laws group also developed a survey that was sent out to previous Ronin Scholars in early November 2024. The summary of the results of the survey was released as part of a presentation by Ruth Duerr in January 2025 (Due to GDPR privacy rules, we can not share the raw results of the survey since it captured personally identifiable information.) In late 2024, the NJ-based organization legally dissolved itself, leaving the creation of a new organization as the only path forward.
    • By-laws drafting: After a lengthy drafting period between November 2024 and March 2025, where any volunteer was welcome to comment on the evolving documents, and after a review of the documents by pro-bono lawyers, the by-laws were deemed to be ready. The bylaws were drafted by a working group of volunteers, with contributions from the current Interim Council, including Ruth Duerr, Rami Saydjari, J De Loatch, Carolyn Sealfon, Alex Lancaster, Elena Fujiwara, and many former Ronin Institute Research Scholars, including Herbert J Bernstein, Arika Virapongse, Daniel Mietchen, Allan Tameshtit, Nick Jackson, Keith Tse, John Larocco, Jorrit Poelen, Tom Buckholtz, Anne Thessen, Rebecca Koskela, and Laure Haak, to whom we owe many thanks.
    • Only those who directly participated in drafting the final version are formally listed, but all feedback was considered. The drafts of the bylaws are available online, and the approved are available in the Records site.
    • Incorporation: In late March of 2025, the by-laws group moved forward with creating a new legal entity with an Interim Council consisting of six volunteers who were regular contributors to the by-laws group. On 23 April 2025, RIIS 2.0 came into existence by filing for incorporation with the State of California, and the bylaws group was dissolved. A new document repository was established for RIIS 2.0, and the work of the bylaws group was archived.
    • After Incorporation: a set of onboarding policies, Belonging, Code of Conduct, Terms of Service, and Privacy were developed so that Fellows could be onboarded with legal protections for the Fellows and the organization.
    • Community input: Throughout the process of the development of RIIS 2.0, prospective members (many from the now-dissolved NJ-based Ronin Institute) were invited to participate in discussions, such as through recurring meetings, notices, and updates posted on a Discord channel and a Google Group account that were set up by and open to joining from former Research Scholars. Those who participated in working groups and meetings contributed directly to the decision-making process.
    How was the incorporation of RIIS 2.0 funded?

    Since we had no external funding, everyone who volunteered to be involved pulled together their existing resources. For example, one interim council member had a free Zoom account from her previous role at the University of Colorado in Boulder (CU Boulder) that she could use to host meetings until such time as we had a separate Zoom account for the organization. Other participants made interest-free loans to RIIS 2.0 by paying for state filing fees, Board insurance, and domain name registration.

    Why was RIIS 2.0 incorporated in California?

    In one of the early surveys, potential members were polled for the location of incorporation. This poll did not include all 50 states, but only a few that made sense to the bylaws team.

    Colorado was included on the survey because:

    • The original Ronin Institute was already in the State of Colorado business database
    • There were many original Ronin Institute Research Scholars in the state

    California was included on the survey because:

    • California has remained the 5th largest economy in the world since 2017
    • There were many former Ronin Institute Research Scholars in the state

    While this  survey showed a slight preference for Colorado, the by-laws group chose  California because:

    • The dissolution of the original Ronin Institute negated the utility of entry in the State of Colorado business database since a new corporation was required. Incorporation in Colorado requires a physical address, but incorporation in California does not, and the new corporation is completely virtual.
    • California has one of the largest nonprofit sectors in the U.S and is quite friendly towards nonprofits.
    • The bylaws team had also benefited from strong legal connections in California (an Interim Council member), but not in Colorado.
    How was the Interim Council chosen?

    The people on the Interim Council are those who initially volunteered to do the hard work of getting the institute incorporated and legally/financially solvent enough to accept members. This is in keeping with the former NJ-based Ronin Institute's policy of Do-ocracy.  Since they are “interim,” however, once there are enough members or after an agreed-upon time for new members to join, then we will hold elections to elect a new Council (please refer to the Bylaws for how this works).

    Transparency and access

    How can I access bylaws, policies, and records?

    Draft bylaws and related materials, which were the primary activity of the bylaws group prior to incorporation, were, and continue to be, publicly shared. In addition, the final bylaws, other policies (such as policies needed for joining RIIS 2.0), and other public-facing records are available at records.ronininstitute.org.  After incorporation, RIIS 2.0 adopted nonprofit best practices for information access and document retention.

    The Secretary maintains the official repository of all records. Fellows have access to non-confidential documents, such as policy drafts, non-confidential Council meeting minutes, meeting notes for Working Groups they belong to, and other approved documents, which are made available through secure online platforms. Fellows     may request inspection of records that are not routinely shared in accordance with the bylaws and internal access protocols..
